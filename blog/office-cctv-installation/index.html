Office CCTV Installation in Lahore

Published 11 November 2026 · SmartShield Security, Lahore

Office CCTV installation in Lahore: what to cover, privacy rules, recorder and access control, costs and how installation works for your office.

What Should Office Cameras Cover?

Offices need coverage that protects staff, visitors and assets while respecting privacy. The areas that matter most are the entrance and reception, corridors, the server room, storage areas and the parking area. Cameras do not belong in washrooms, prayer rooms or private offices — and staff should know where cameras are and why.

Planning an Office System

Start with a walkthrough of the office: entrances, reception, corridors, meeting rooms, server and storage areas. Count the cameras per area, then choose the recorder — an NVR with IP cameras suits most offices, because it gives high resolution and room to grow. Recording access should be limited to the people you authorise, and retention is usually 15–30 days.

What Does Office CCTV Cost?

Office systems cost more than home systems because they have more cameras and longer cable runs. In Lahore, CCTV installation usually ranges between PKR 12,500 and PKR 50,000 or more, and larger offices go above that range. The exact price depends on camera count, resolution, recorder, storage and installation complexity, and is confirmed after a site assessment.

Installation Without Disrupting Work

Office installation is often scheduled outside working hours or in stages, so your team can keep working. Cabling is run neatly, cameras are mounted securely, and the recorder is placed somewhere safe and ventilated. Mobile viewing is set up for the authorised staff, with a short training walkthrough.

Combining CCTV with Attendance

Many offices install a biometric attendance machine alongside CCTV — cameras cover entrances and common areas, while the attendance machine handles staff records. Ask us about a combined office package.

What to Check Before Signing Off

Before the installation is signed off, check three things: every camera shows a clear live picture, playback works for recordings from earlier in the day, and the authorised staff can view the system on their phones. If any of these fail, it is fixed on the spot — not discovered weeks later when footage is actually needed. Keep a note of who has recording access, and review that list whenever staff change roles or leave.

Privacy Rules Worth Knowing

Staff should know where cameras are and why they are there. Cameras belong in entrances, corridors and common areas — not in washrooms, prayer rooms or private offices. A short written note telling staff what is recorded and who can view it prevents most misunderstandings, and it is good practice for any business.
