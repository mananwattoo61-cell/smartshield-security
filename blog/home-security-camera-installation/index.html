Home Security Camera Installation in Lahore

Published 11 November 2026 · SmartShield Security, Lahore

Home security camera installation in Lahore: where to place cameras, which type to choose, costs, mobile viewing and how installation works.

Where Should Home Cameras Go?

Start with the entry points: the main gate, the front door, the driveway and any side entrance. Then cover the boundary walls and the back of the house. Indoor cameras are only needed where you ask for them — a main hallway or living area — and never in bedrooms or bathrooms. The goal is to see who approaches and enters, not to watch the family.

Which Cameras Suit a Home?

Most Lahore homes use a mix: bullet cameras outdoors for the gate, driveway and boundary, and dome cameras indoors or at the entrance for wide, discreet coverage. Night vision matters for outdoor cameras, because most incidents happen after dark. For a typical house, 4 cameras cover the essentials; larger houses and villas usually need 6 or 8.

What Does Home Installation Cost?

Home CCTV installation in Lahore typically starts at PKR 12,500 for a basic wireless setup, and most home installations fall between PKR 12,500 and PKR 50,000 or more depending on camera count, type and storage. A 4MP camera typically costs PKR 6,000–12,000, with installation separate. Your final price is confirmed after a site assessment — use the Smart Calculator for a preliminary estimate.

Viewing on Your Phone

Remote viewing is configured as part of installation where your system supports it. You can check the gate while you are at work, review recordings if something happens, and share access with family members. We set it up on your own phone and show you how to use it before we leave.

Keeping Your Home System Working

A home system needs very little care, but a little care goes a long way: wipe camera lenses every month or two, check that the recorder is still recording every week, and keep the recorder somewhere safe and ventilated. If a camera goes offline or the app stops connecting, check the power supply and the Wi-Fi first — those two causes cover most home faults.
