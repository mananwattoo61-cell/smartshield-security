Bullet vs Dome CCTV Cameras: Which One Do You Need?

Published 11 November 2026 · SmartShield Security, Lahore

Bullet vs dome CCTV cameras compared: coverage, range, mounting, night vision and where each type works best in a Lahore home or shop.

What Is a Bullet Camera?

A bullet camera is the long, cylindrical camera most people picture when they think of CCTV. It is designed for outdoor use and long-range coverage — boundaries, driveways, gates and parking areas. Its shape makes it visible, which itself deters intruders, and its lens is built for distance.

What Is a Dome Camera?

A dome camera sits in a rounded, discreet housing, usually mounted on a ceiling. It gives wide-angle coverage and is hard to tell exactly where it is pointing, which makes it popular indoors — shops, offices, hallways and living areas. Outdoor dome cameras exist too, but they are best for shorter distances.

Bullet vs Dome at a Glance

  • Range: bullet cameras see further; dome cameras cover a wider angle at short range.
  • Placement: bullet cameras suit walls and outdoor boundaries; dome cameras suit ceilings and indoor spaces.
  • Visibility: bullet cameras are an obvious deterrent; dome cameras are discreet.
  • Weather: both come in weatherproof versions — check the rating before buying for outdoor use.
  • Night vision: both types offer infrared night vision; the effective range depends on the model.

Which One for Your Property?

Most Lahore homes and shops need both: bullet cameras for the gate, driveway and boundary, and dome cameras for the entrance, shop floor or hallway. A single type rarely covers everything well. When we plan a system, we mix the two — bullet where distance matters, dome where wide, discreet coverage matters.

A Note on Image Quality

Shape is only half the story. Resolution, lens quality and lighting matter just as much. A well-placed 2MP camera beats a badly placed 4K camera every time, because the angle and the light decide what the footage actually shows.

What About PTZ Cameras?

For very large areas — a warehouse yard, a big parking area or a large boundary — a PTZ camera that can pan, tilt and zoom covers more ground than a fixed bullet or dome. PTZ cameras cost more and suit active monitoring rather than set-and-forget recording, so they are usually added alongside fixed cameras rather than replacing them. For most homes and shops, a mix of bullet and dome cameras is enough.
